The Start menu

Click the firox button (or tap the Super key) to open the Start menu. The left side has your pinned programs and the ones you use most. The right side opens your folders, Settings, the Bazaar app store and system tools. All Programs lists everything installed, grouped into folders; you can also just start typing to search.

To change the picture at the top of the Start menu, click it (or open Settings → User Accounts) and pick one of the built-in pictures or Browse for more pictures… to use your own photo. The same picture appears next to your name on the welcome screen when you log on.

The desktop

The wallpaper is on every monitor; the icons (Computer, your home folder, Network, the Recycle Bin and everything in your Desktop folder) are on the main display. Double-click an icon to open it. Programs such as AppImages run when you double-click them; a downloaded one asks once whether you want to run it, then remembers (or tick Allow running this file as a program in its Properties). Drag icons to put them where you like (they stay there), onto a folder to move files into it, onto the Recycle Bin to delete them, or into another program. Drop files from other programs onto the desktop to copy them there (hold Shift to move them instead).

Right-click an icon to open it with another program, cut, copy, rename (or press F2), move it to the Recycle Bin (or press Delete) or see its properties. Right-click the desktop to arrange the icons by name, type, size or date, paste, or make a new folder or text document. To change the wallpaper, its position and colour, or which icons are shown, right-click the desktop and choose Desktop Background (also in Settings).

Locking your computer

Press Super+L (the Windows key and L) to lock your computer when you step away, or let Settings → Power Options lock it after a while. The lock screen shows your picture, the time and the date; type your password and press Enter to carry on where you left off. If Caps Lock is on, it says so. Turn off computer at the bottom lets you sleep, turn off or restart without unlocking (unsaved work in open programs is lost if you turn off or restart).

Files and folders (Firox Explorer)

Firox Explorer opens your files and folders: double-click Computer or your home folder on the desktop, press Super+E, or open it from the Start menu. The sidebar has your folders, your drives (click one to open it; right-click to eject it) and network places. Type in the search box to find things in the folder you're in; click Subfolders to search every folder inside it too (Search in the Start menu opens it that way for your home folder).

Connect to Server (in its sidebar, under Network) connects to a Windows shared folder, an SFTP, FTP or WebDAV server, or cloud storage (Amazon S3 and compatible services, Backblaze B2). The connection is only for you and lasts until you disconnect or log off. Tick Remember to keep the password in your keyring (it's unlocked when you log in), and Connect when I log in to have it ready every time. Right-click a connection to disconnect, edit or remove it.

You can also browse: open Network in the sidebar (or type smb://server in the address bar) and double-click a computer, then a shared folder. You're asked for a user name and password if it needs one.

If a connection doesn't work, the error message shows what went wrong, and S3 and B2 connections keep a log in ~/.local/state/firox/mounts. Right-click the connection and choose Test Connection (or run /usr/libexec/firox/firox-explorer --test "name" in a terminal). It checks each step, from your keyring to listing the bucket and trying a mount, and saves a report next to the log. Passwords and keys are replaced by *** in the report and the logs, but read it through before you share it.

The menus at the top of the window: File (new window, folder or text document, open, rename, delete, properties), Edit (cut, copy, paste, select all or none, invert the selection, copy a file's location), View (Icons or Details, sort by name, size, type or date, hidden files, and showing or hiding the toolbar, sidebar and status bar), Go (back, forward, up, your folders, the Recycle Bin), Bookmarks, Tools (Connect to Server, Disconnect All, options) and Help (including Keyboard Shortcuts). Press Alt with the underlined letter, or F10, to open them from the keyboard. Bookmarks → Add Bookmark (Ctrl+D) adds the folder you're in to the sidebar and to every program's Open and Save windows. Your view and sort choices are remembered.

The taskbar

Each open window has a button on the taskbar. Click it to switch to the window, or click it again to minimise it. Right-click it for Restore, Minimize, Maximize and Close; a middle-click closes it.

When a program has two or more windows open, they share one button that shows how many there are, such as 3 Firefox. Click it to choose a window, or right-click it to minimise or close them all. To give every window its own button, right-click an empty part of the taskbar and untick Group similar windows. With more than one monitor, each one has its own taskbar showing the windows on it.

The small icons next to the firox button are Quick Launch: one click starts the program. To add one, right-click a program in the Start menu (or its button on the taskbar) and choose Pin to Quick Launch; right-click an icon to remove it. Drag icons and window buttons to put them in the order you like.

The icons by the clock: apps that put an icon there, network, Bluetooth, the eye (click to keep the screen on: no screen saver, screen off or sleep until you click it again or log off), volume, battery, an update waiting for a restart, and the notification bell. Click the battery for the time left, screen brightness and the power mode: Power saver makes the battery last longer, Performance makes the computer faster.

To make the taskbar hide itself when you're not using it, show seconds on the clock, or hide Quick Launch, open Settings → Taskbar (or right-click an empty part of the taskbar). Settings → Default Programs chooses which program opens web links, email, music, videos, pictures, text files and PDFs.

If the taskbar or the desktop icons and wallpaper ever vanish, for example when a monitor wakes up, they come back by themselves within a few seconds. If they don't, log off and back on. Each time this happens it's noted in ~/.local/state/firox/session.log.

Copy and paste

Copy with Ctrl+C and paste with Ctrl+V. In the Command Prompt, use Ctrl+Shift+C and Ctrl+Shift+V (or Ctrl+Insert and Shift+Insert), because Ctrl+C stops the running program there. What you copy stays available even after you close the program you copied it from, and it's shared with Android apps.

Press Super+V to see what you copied recently, including pictures. Pick an item (or type to search) and it's ready to paste. The history is cleared when you log off; change that, or turn the history off, in Settings → Clipboard. Passwords copied from password managers aren't remembered.

Screenshots and the Snipping Tool

Press Super+Shift+S and drag a rectangle to snip part of the screen (press Esc to cancel). The snip is copied, ready to paste with Ctrl+V, and opens in the Snipping Tool, where you can draw on it with the pen or highlighter and then Copy, Save (to Pictures/Screenshots) or Save As. Open the Snipping Tool from All Programs to snip the whole screen or one monitor, or to wait a few seconds first (for example, to open a menu).

Print Screen saves the whole screen straight to your Pictures folder.

Battery

On a laptop you're warned when the battery is low (10%) and again when it's critically low (5%). At 3% the computer goes to sleep after a 15-second warning, so you don't lose your work; plug it in to stop it. Change the levels, or choose hibernate or shut down instead, in Settings → Power Options.

Notifications

Messages from programs pop up in the bottom-right corner for a few seconds. To see ones you missed, click the bell in the notification area. Ones that arrived since you last looked are highlighted. Click a notification to open the program that sent it, or remove it with ×.

Turn on Do not disturb in that panel (or right-click the bell) to stop the pop-ups. Notifications are still kept in the panel, and urgent ones such as a low battery warning still appear.

Mouse, touchpad and keyboard

Settings → Mouse changes pointer speed, double-click speed, scrolling direction and speed, the pointer size, left-handed buttons, and on laptops the touchpad (tap to click, two-finger scrolling, turning it off while typing or when a mouse is plugged in).

Settings → Keyboard changes the key repeat speed, turns on Num Lock when you log in, and sets your keyboard layouts. With more than one layout, switch between them with Alt+Shift (you can pick another shortcut).

Web apps

Firox Linux turns websites into apps with PWAsForFirefox. Each web app gets its own window, taskbar button and Start menu entry, and runs in its own Firefox profile.

  1. Open the site in Firefox.
  2. Click the PWAsForFirefox toolbar button (in the puzzle-piece menu if it's hidden).
  3. Choose Install. The app appears in Start → All Programs → Web Apps.

Manage installed apps in Start → Web Apps. The first time you log in, Firox Linux downloads the web app runtime in the background. If it didn't finish, run ujust install-webapp-runtime.

Android apps

Android apps run in Waydroid. The first time you open Start → Android Apps, Firox Linux downloads the Android image for you. You can also run it yourself:

ujust setup-waydroidAndroid without Google apps
ujust setup-waydroid gappsAndroid with the Play Store
ujust waydroid-google-idGet the ID to register a Play Store install
ujust reset-waydroidRemove Android and all its data

Installed Android apps show up in All Programs → Android Apps. Waydroid needs a GPU it supports. NVIDIA cards with the proprietary driver usually don't work.

NVIDIA graphics

If your PC has an NVIDIA graphics card (GTX 16 / RTX 20 series or newer), run ujust install-nvidia in a terminal. It switches Firox to a version with NVIDIA's driver built in and keeps your files and settings. Restart afterwards. ujust remove-nvidia switches back.

If Secure Boot is turned on, run ujust enroll-secure-boot-key before restarting, then choose Enroll MOK on the blue screen at the next boot and enter universalblue.

No sound?

Click the speaker in the notification area for a volume slider, Mute, and a list of speakers and headphones to play through. Then check Settings → Sounds and Audio Devices: make sure the right output is selected and nothing is muted.

Some PCs with Realtek onboard audio stay silent or crackle on Linux. Settings → Sound Troubleshooter runs linux-audio-fix, which detects your chip, shows what it will change and asks before doing anything. It turns off Auto-Mute, sends the chip's startup commands, and sets them to be re-applied at every boot. It saves a backup first.

ujust fix-realtek-audioGuided fix
ujust fix-realtek-audio --dry-runPreview only, changes nothing
ujust fix-realtek-audio --list-chipsShow detected audio chips
ujust fix-realtek-audio --restorePut the saved mixer settings back
ujust fix-realtek-audio --uninstallStop re-applying the fix at boot

It only applies to Realtek HD-Audio chips. USB audio (such as ALC4080 on some motherboards) and newer Intel laptops that use SOF firmware need different fixes; the tool tells you if that's your case.

Installing software

Firox Linux is an image-based system: the OS itself is read-only and updates as one piece. Install apps as web apps, Android apps or Flatpaks (Start → Bazaar). For command-line tools, use a toolbox or distrobox container.

Updates

The system updates itself in the background, and the new version takes effect when you restart. When an update is waiting, an icon appears in the notification area: click it to restart, or right-click it to see what's new. To update now, open Settings → System Update or run ujust update.

Desktop Gadgets

The Sidebar on the right of the desktop holds gadgets: a clock, today's date, CPU and memory meters, the weather, a slide show of your Pictures folder, a sticky note, Now Playing (the song in your music or video player, with play/pause, next and previous), a Network Meter, Feed Headlines from a news site, a Timer, a sliding Picture Puzzle, Currency conversion and Disk Space. Click + Gadgets at the top to add one; hover over a gadget and click its × to remove it. Right-click a gadget for its Options (for the weather, type your city and pick °C or °F; for Feed Headlines, the feed's address; for the Timer, how many minutes; for Currency, the amount and currencies; for the Picture Puzzle, a new puzzle) or to move it up or down.

Click the clock or calendar for the full calendar, the CPU meter for the Task Manager, a headline to read it, the Timer to start or pause it, and Disk Space to see your drives. To use a picture of your own in the puzzle, put it in your Pictures folder. To turn the Sidebar off, right-click it and choose Hide the Sidebar (just for now) or untick it in Settings → Taskbar; Desktop Gadgets in the Start menu brings it back.

Wi-Fi and networks

Click the network icon by the clock. Use the switch at the top to turn Wi-Fi on or off. Click a network to connect to it; if it has a password, type it in the box that appears and press Enter. Click the network you're on to disconnect, or Forget it so the computer stops joining it. Airplane mode turns off Wi-Fi and Bluetooth together. For a network that hides its name click Hidden network…; for work or university networks that need a username or certificate, and for VPNs and fixed addresses, click Network Connections….

Bluetooth

Click the Bluetooth icon by the clock. To connect headphones, a mouse, a speaker or a phone for the first time, click Add a device…, put the device into pairing mode (often by holding its Bluetooth or power button), then click it in the list and click Pair. If a code appears, check it matches the one on the device. After that, click the device to connect or disconnect it; it also reconnects by itself when it's turned on nearby. Remove forgets it.

The Run box

Press Super+R (or Start → Run…) and type a program, a folder such as ~/Downloads, a file, a web address or a command, then press Enter. As you type, the rest of a matching name is filled in and selected: keep typing to replace it, or press Enter to accept it. The arrow lists what you ran before. Tick Run in a terminal window to see a command's output. Familiar Windows names work too, such as cmd, notepad, calc, control, taskmgr and msconfig.

Programs that start when you log in

Open Settings → Startup Programs (or type msconfig in Run). Click Add… to pick a program from the Start menu or type your own command. Untick a program to stop it starting without removing it from the list. You can also turn off Desktop Gadgets here, or turn on the classic network (nm-applet) and Bluetooth (Blueman) tray icons if you need them, for example for a VPN that asks for its password every time. Changes take effect the next time you log in.

Backing up your files

Open Settings → Backup and Restore, click Choose where… and pick a USB drive (or type a network folder such as smb://nas/backups). From then on Firox Linux backs up your home folder every hour while the drive is connected, and straight away when you plug it in. Each backup only takes the space of what changed, and older backups are thinned out (hourly for a day, daily for a month, then weekly) as the drive fills.

To get something back, click Restore files…, pick a backup by its date and time, select the files or folders and click Restore selected; a restored copy that would overwrite something gets "(restored)" in its name. Your backups are ordinary folders in "Firox Backups" on the drive, so you can also open them on any computer.

Syncing folders between your computers

Turn on Settings → Sync on each Firox computer at home. They find each other on the network; click Pair… next to the other computer and type the PIN it shows. Tick the folders to keep the same (Documents, Pictures and so on): a file you save on one appears on the others within moments. Syncing happens directly between your computers on your own network, never through the internet. If another computer deletes or replaces a file, the old one is kept for 30 days in a hidden .stversions folder.

Sync isn't a backup: deleting a file deletes it everywhere. Use Backup and Restore as well.

Going back after an update (System Restore)

Each update installs a complete new version of Firox Linux and keeps the one you used before. If something stopped working after an update, open Settings → System Restore, pick Previous version and click Restore this version; it starts the next time you restart (Undo changes your mind). Your files, settings and apps aren't touched.

Automatic updates would bring the newest version back the next day, so click Pause automatic updates there to stay on the restored one, and resume them once the problem is fixed. Keep this version holds on to a version you trust even after later updates; kept versions can also be chosen in the boot menu (hold Shift or press Esc while the computer starts).

Task Manager

Press Ctrl+Alt+Del (or Ctrl+Shift+Esc). Applications lists your open windows: End Task asks a stuck one to close. Processes shows everything that's running and how much CPU and memory it uses; click a column to sort, and End Process stops one that won't close. Performance graphs CPU and memory use.

Screen resolution and monitors

The main display. With more than one monitor, one of them is your main display. It has the full taskbar with its icons and clock, the Sidebar and notifications; the others extend your desktop, each with a taskbar showing the windows on it, the start button and the clock. To choose, open Settings → Display, click a monitor (or pick it from the list), tick Make this my main display and click Apply. Identify shows each monitor's number on it. Each combination of monitors remembers its own main display. To have one taskbar, on the main display only, untick Show the taskbar on all displays in Settings → Taskbar.

Resolution and arrangement. In Settings → Display, click Resolution and arrangement, change resolution, refresh rate, scale or arrangement, and close the window. Your layout is saved when you close it and comes back at every login. Each combination of monitors (for example, laptop alone vs. docked) remembers its own layout. If you write your own ~/.config/kanshi/config, Firox leaves it alone. If a layout doesn't come back, the details are in ~/.local/state/firox/displays.log.

Customizing

The desktop is labwc (window manager), firox-panel (taskbar; add your own styling in ~/.config/firox/panel.css) and firox-desktop (desktop icons and wallpaper). Run ujust customize-desktop to copy the defaults into ~/.config, where you can edit them. ujust reset-desktop puts the defaults back.
