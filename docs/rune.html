Meet Rune

Rune is the Firox mascot: a fox-dragon with a fox's curiosity, a dragon's patience and a tail that has never quite stopped burning. He keeps the stones that Firox is carved into.

The spark

Every year, millions of computers are thrown away while they still work. Rune began on the last night of one of them: an old family desktop, put out by the curb with its cable wrapped around it. Somebody switched it on one final time to wipe it, and as the screen faded, a single spark jumped from the power button and didn't come down.

It drifted for a long time, through cables and air and the spaces between them, until it settled in the Glade of Standing Stones, a quiet place deep in the network where old things go to be remembered. It landed in the moss at the foot of the stones and curled up to keep warm. By morning it was a small blue fox with a flame where its tail should be.

The horns came the following spring and the wing the year after. Dragons, as the stones like to say, grow into themselves.

Keeper of the stones

At the heart of the glade is a ring of runestones, and into every stone a whole computer system is carved, rune by rune. The first thing the young fox learned was to read them, which is how he got his name.

Carved stone doesn't change. No stray program can scratch it, and nothing on it slowly rots. That's how Firox works, too: each version of the system is a complete, finished image that nothing on your computer can alter. When a new version is ready, Rune carves a fresh stone overnight, and the next time you restart, your computer simply steps across onto it.

Now and then a new stone has a crack in it. When that happens Rune doesn't argue. He walks you back to yesterday's stone, which is still standing exactly where you left it. In Firox that's System Restore, and it's why updates can't break your computer.

The crystals and the scroll

The glowing crystals at the edge of the glade hold memories. Rune tucks a copy of what matters into them (your documents, your photos, your settings) so that nothing is ever really lost. You'll know them as Backup and Restore and Sync.

The scroll is older than Rune is. It holds the rules a school, a library or an office writes for its computers. Every so often Rune unrolls it, reads it aloud to every computer in his care, and makes sure they listen. That's the Policy Editor, and he's quite strict about it.

The tail

Rune's flame is orange on the outside and blue at the heart: warm for the familiar things, like a taskbar at the bottom and a Start menu where you left it, and blue for what's new underneath.

When your computer starts, the little light circling under his picture on the boot screen is the last ember of his tail, going round and round while he wakes everything up. It has never once gone out. When you shut down, he tucks it under his chin and waits.

Rune, in short

Species
Fox-dragon. As far as anyone knows, the only one.

About as big as a house cat, with the wingspan of an open Start menu.

In the Glade of Standing Stones, and on your desktop.

Rune on your desktop

Rune lives in a corner of your desktop, in a little window onto his glade. He isn't a still picture: he breathes and blinks, his tail throws embers, the runes and crystals glow, the stars twinkle, and the glade tilts a little when your mouse is over him.

Click him to chat. Ask him anything: how to do something in Firox, what a setting means, or something about the wider world. He knows Firox's help by heart and can search the web for anything recent.

He can do things for you, but he always asks first. Before he does anything on your computer, a card pops up saying exactly what he wants to do, with Allow and Not now. He can:

  • open Settings, at the right page
  • open Firox's tools (Task Manager, Disk Cleanup, Backup and Restore…)
  • open your apps, folders or a web page
  • switch the look
  • turn Night Light on or off
  • set the volume
  • check for updates
  • start a backup

He can't run commands, read your files or see your screen.

How he talks: Claude, with your own key

Rune thinks with Claude, Anthropic's AI. To chat, he needs your own Claude API key, from the Claude Console. You pay Anthropic directly for what you use, usually a few cents a day of chatting. In his settings you can pick a cheaper, faster model.

No spyware.

  • Nothing until you ask: nothing leaves your computer until you've given him a key and asked him something.
  • What's sent: only what you type goes to Anthropic, together with Firox's help so he knows Firox. It goes straight from your computer, with no Firox server in between and no tracking.
  • When it's sent: a small network icon shows while a request is on its way.
  • Your key: kept in your keyring.
  • Your conversation: stays on your computer until you start a new one.

Right-click him for his settings:

  • which corner he sits in, and his size
  • his glade or just him
  • above or under your windows
  • which model, and your key
  • Put Rune Away; open Rune from the Start menu to bring him back

Schools and offices can turn him off, or keep him as a mascot who never chats, with a policy.

Where to find him

  • The corner of your desktop, in his glade: click him to chat.
  • The boot screen, above the firox name, with his ember circling underneath.
  • The Start button, in every look.
  • The Start menu, at the top right, across from your picture.
  • The wallpaper: Rune's Glade, the default in every look. It's also in Desktop Background.
  • The Command Prompt: type fastfetch and he sits next to your computer's details.
  • This website, in the corner of every page.
